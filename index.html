MASTER FILE // ACCESS PROPOSAL

Keep the Library Light On

1 At 4:15 p.m., our school library closes even though many students remain on campus for tutoring, rehearsals, practices, club meetings, or rides that do not arrive until much later. Some try to finish assignments in hallways where outlets are scarce. Others wait outside or move from one activity to another because there is no supervised place left to read or work. That gap matters.

2 The problem is not a lack of space. The library already has tables, internet access, charging stations, reference materials, and the books students need. The challenge is supervision. Keeping the entire building open would require more staff than the school can reasonably provide, but closing every study space at the final bell leaves students with no dependable option between the end of class and the time they can leave campus.

3 There is a practical way forward. The school could pilot after-school library hours on Tuesdays and Thursdays until 5:30 p.m. for six weeks. One staff member could supervise a single study zone while students sign in and remain in that area. Limiting the pilot to two afternoons would control staffing costs without requiring the school to open every classroom or extend the entire school day.

4 A pilot would also produce useful evidence. Leaders could track how many students use the space, when demand is highest, what supervision is actually required, and whether students use the time for tutoring or independent work. If few students participate, the school could end the program without a large commitment. If the room is consistently full, leaders would have evidence for expanding it.

5 The school already asks students to remain after the bell for learning, practice, performance, and service. Providing our students one reliable place to read, work, and wait safely would not solve every after-school problem. It would, however, test a focused solution to a gap the school has the power to address.
